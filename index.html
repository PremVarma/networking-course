Networking for Backend Engineers

You already write Spring Boot controllers, Node.js services, React apps and FastAPI endpoints. This course explains everything that happens underneath them — from electrical pulses on a wire to Kubernetes Ingress — building intuition first, technical depth second.

By the end you will be able to trace a single browser click through every cable, router, load balancer, proxy, and pod it touches — and answer interview questions about each step.

How this course teaches

Every chapter follows the same rules: why before how, real-world analogies, ASCII packet diagrams, examples from your actual stack (Spring Boot, Node.js, React, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis, Kafka, Docker, Kubernetes, AWS), and — after every major topic — explanations at three depths plus interview questions and misconception-busting. Curriculum

Read in order — every chapter builds on the previous ones. Budget roughly 45–90 minutes per part.

PART 01

Why Networking Exists

What a network is, why computers need them, the fundamental problems of communication, what physically travels on a wire, LAN/WAN/MAN, clients and servers.

 PART 02

The OSI Model

The 7-layer master map the rest of the course hangs on: every layer's job, protocols, devices, headers, plus encapsulation and a full packet journey.

 PART 03

IP Addresses (Layer 3)

IPv4, IPv6, public vs private, CIDR and subnet math, NAT, routers and routing tables, gateways, ARP — and how your laptop finds Google.

 PART 04

TCP & UDP (Layer 4)

Reliability, ordering, acknowledgements, the three-way handshake, retransmission, flow and congestion control, windowing — and when UDP wins.

 PART 05

Ports & Sockets

Why 8080, 5432, 6379 and 9092 exist: sockets, the 5-tuple, client vs server ports, and the well-known ports you will meet every day.

 PART 06

DNS

The Internet's phonebook: root, TLD and authoritative servers, recursive resolvers, caching, TTL, and every record type from A to PTR.

 PART 07

HTTP

The request–response model, statelessness, methods, status codes, headers, cookies, sessions, authentication — with raw HTTP messages.

 PART 08

HTTPS & TLS/SSL

Encryption from zero: symmetric vs asymmetric keys, the TLS handshake, certificates and certificate authorities, and a full visit to amazon.com.

 PART 09

Load Balancers

Horizontal scaling and high availability: Layer 4 vs Layer 7 balancing, health checks, routing algorithms, AWS ALB and NLB.

 PART 10

Reverse Proxies

Forward vs reverse proxies, why every serious deployment has one, and Nginx/HAProxy/Traefik in front of your Spring Boot app.

 PART 11

Cloud Networking (AWS)

VPCs, subnets, route tables, internet and NAT gateways, security groups vs NACLs — and a packet's full path from browser to EC2.

 PART 12

Docker & Kubernetes Networking

Bridge, host and overlay networks; pod IPs, Services, ClusterIP, NodePort, LoadBalancer and Ingress — and how they map to everything above.

 PART 13

Microservices Networking

REST vs gRPC vs message queues, service discovery, API gateways, and service meshes with Istio and Envoy.

 PART 14

Complete Request Journeys

Five end-to-end walkthroughs — localhost, ALB+ECS, Kubernetes Ingress, Nginx, CDN — with every layer, protocol, header and device.

 PART 15

Backend Engineer Mastery

Everything connected into one mental model: the full OSI mapping table, recurring design themes, a debugging playbook, and an interview gauntlet.

How to get the most out of it

  • Read linearly. Part 4 assumes Part 3; Part 12 assumes Parts 9–11. The course never uses a term before defining it.
  • Answer interview questions out loud before clicking. The collapsible answers are written the way a strong candidate would actually respond.
  • Run the commands. When a chapter shows curl -v, dig, ss, or docker network inspect, run it against your own services — the output will match what you just read.
  • Draw the diagrams from memory. If you can reproduce Fig 0.1 above with all its labels after Part 15, you have the mental model.
